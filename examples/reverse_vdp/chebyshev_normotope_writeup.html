The ChebyshevNormotope, piece by piece

What the set is, why the basis is Chebyshev, what the Carleman lift and its residual look like, how a degree-m set is initialized from linear data and why the seeding scale is load-bearing, how the symplectic step and the refit work, and how the four offset bounds relate. File: immrax/parametric/sets/chebyshev_normotope.py; tests: tests/test_chebyshev.py.

1The set

S = { ox + z : ‖α b(T z)‖₂² ≤ y },    w := T z ∈ [-1,1]ⁿ

The shaping map is the composite α ∘ b ∘ T — directly analogous to the linear normotope’s {x : ‖H(x − ox)‖ ≤ y}, with the fixed nonlinearity b sandwiched between two learned linear maps.

  • b(w) — the vector of multivariate Chebyshev products T_β(w) = ∏ᵢ T_{βᵢ}(wᵢ) over all multi-indices of total degree |β| ≤ m, including the constant T₀ = 1. Size |b| = C(n+m, m). Including T₀ matters: Chebyshev products generate constants (w² = (T₂+T₀)/2), and without T₀ those constants leak into the residual at O(L²) — a low-order error that never shrinks with lift order.
  • T — a full n×n preconditioner (not a per-axis scale): it maps the deviation z into scaled coordinates w = Tz where the basis is evaluated. The refit (§6) updates T every step so the set’s linear marginal is the unit ball — the frame follows the set’s rotation and stretch, keeping the basis well-conditioned. (A diagonal-only scale could not follow rotation; cond(α) blew up and the flow diverged.)
  • α — dense |b|×|b|, invertible. Row i defines one “certificate coordinate” (a polynomial αᵢᶱb(w)); the set is the sublevel set of the sum of their squares.
  • y — the squared level (unlike the other normotopes, where ‖·‖ ≤ y). Consequence: the offset ODE has no 1/(2y) singularity as the set contracts — y can pass through 0 harmlessly.

2Why Chebyshev at all

The monomial-basis sets (PolynomialNormotope, GramNormotope) all stumble on the same step: bounding a polynomial expression over the current set. Monomial interval arithmetic degrades with degree (zⁱ ranges compound), so the drift bound is either loose (log-norm), grid-priced (partition), or solver-priced (SOS). On the fitted box every Chebyshev term obeys

|T_β(w)| ≤ 1   exactly, for every β, every degree,

so for any polynomial written in Chebyshev coefficients, sup₁₋₁₁₁₋ p ≤ c₀ + Σ|c_β| — one abs-sum, degree-independent. Measured: this abs-sum is within ~10% of the true box range on our benchmarks. The catch (§7): it is a box bound, and the drift sup lives on the set.

3Computing the linearization A and the leftover C_H

In scaled coordinates a trajectory’s deviation obeys ẋ = T(f(ox + T⁻¹w) − f(ox)), so by the chain rule each entry of the basis vector moves as

d/dt bᵢ(w) = (∂bᵢ/∂w)·ẋ =: pᵢ(w)

For polynomial f, each pᵢ is just some polynomial in w of total degree ≤ m + deg(f) − 1, so it has a unique, exact, finite expansion in Chebyshev polynomials: pᵢ(w) = Σ_β cᵢβ T_β(w). That is all A and C_H are: the coefficient table of the lifted vector field in the Chebyshev basis, split at degree m — coefficients on terms of degree ≤ m form row i of A (the part expressible in the basis we carry), coefficients on higher terms form row i of C_H (the leftover, which is what drives y).

The only question is how to compute the numbers cᵢβ, since they depend on ox and T and are recomputed every step. The method is the classical evaluate-then-transform trick:

  • A 1-D polynomial of degree ≤ q−1 is uniquely determined by its values at q points — the same way a parabola through 3 points is that parabola, exactly.
  • If the q points are the roots of T_q (“Chebyshev nodes”, xⱼ = cos(π(2j+1)/2q)), the map from values at the nodes to Chebyshev coefficients has a closed form, cₖ = (2−δₖ₀)/q · Σⱼ p(xⱼ)cos(kθⱼ), because Tₖ(cosθ) = cos(kθ) — Chebyshev polynomials sampled at these nodes are literally sampled cosines, and cosines of different frequencies are orthogonal over the nodes. This values→coefficients map is the Discrete Cosine Transform (the JPEG transform); at our sizes it is a small q×q matrix multiply.
  • In n dimensions: evaluate pᵢ on the tensor grid of qⁿ node points, apply the 1-D matrix along each axis. Choosing q = m + deg f makes the uniqueness apply, so the recovered coefficients are exact — exact polynomial algebra carried out numerically, not a fit.

Per step this is a few dozen function evaluations (autodiff supplies ∂b/∂w) plus two small matrix multiplies. The alternatives are worse: symbolic product-formula expansion is per-step bookkeeping, and routing through the monomial basis reintroduces the 2ᵗ coefficient blow-ups this class exists to avoid. deg f is detected once by a Taylor probe (polynomial f only, for now — for general smooth f the expansion would need a certified truncation remainder).

The structure of C_H that everything else relies on (verified numerically): it is a ladder with almost all the error on the top rung. d/dt of a degree-k basis function under a degree-d field has degree ≤ k+d−1, which exceeds m only for k near the top — so Hₖ = 0 for all low degrees; and where nonzero its size is O(Lm+1−k) (L = physical set size), because the field’s degree-j terms in w carry Lj−1. Low rungs: ~Lᵘ, negligible. Top rung (k = m): ~m²L, dominant.

4Initialization: lifting a linear set to degree m

The initial data is linear: a center and a box/ellipsoid — degree-1 information. The class needs a full |b|×|b| invertible α₀. The linear rows are dictated by the data (they encode ‖w‖² ≤ 1, the circumscribed ellipsoid). Every other row is not determined by the set at all: appending a row ε·(basis function)ᶱ adds ε²T_β(w)² to V, and compensating in y makes the represented set the same up to an O(ε²) inflation. The rows must be nonzero (α⁻¹ is needed by the refit and the hull), but their magnitudes are free. So initialization is really one question: at what magnitudes do you seed the higher-degree rows of a representation whose data only specifies degree 1? (This is the same freedom as choosing the regularizer blocks Bₖ in PolynomialNormotope.)

The seeding rule follows from the normalization of the basis. In the refit coordinates every basis function is O(1) on the set, so the natural geometric grading — degree-k quantities are Lᵗ-small, which is what makes a truncation at degree m an O(Lm+1) error in monomial coordinates — lives nowhere unless α carries it. Seed the degree-k rows at

εₖ = eps · gammak−1,    gamma ≈ the effective expansion parameter (≈ set scale × field curvature)

and the truncation error is priced at gamma2(m−1): raising m then behaves like raising the order of a Taylor model — pure accuracy, cost falling geometrically (measured drift rates 0.0045 → 0.0003 → ≤10⁻⁵ for m = 2, 3, 4). The price is cond(α₀) ~ gamma−(m−1), which is mild.

The naive seeding — all rows at the same ε — produces the same initial set but catastrophically different dynamics. To see why, the whole mechanism fits in one 1-D computation. Basis (1, w, T₂), field ẋ = c₂w², seeded α = diag(ε₀, 1, ε₂):

d/dt T₀ = 0,   d/dt T₁ = c₂(T₂+T₀)/2   ← representable, cancelled by the adjoint
d/dt T₂ = 3c₂T₁ + c₂T₃   ← T₃ is outside the basis: the only un-cancellable term
⇒  V̇_uncancelled = 2·[ε₂T₂]·[ε₂c₂T₃]  ⇒  |V̇| ≤ 2ε₂²c₂

The seed of the degree-2 row multiplies the one error term — squared. Equal seeding (ε₂ = 0.3) prices it at 0.18c₂; degree-scaled seeding (ε₂ = 0.06) at 0.007c₂. And at each higher m the erroneous slot moves up one degree with a modestly larger derivative factor, so equal seeding makes the cost grow with m (measured: 0.11 → 0.22 → 0.60 → 2.46) while degree-scaled seeding shrinks it geometrically.

The constants. The defaults eps = 0.3, gamma = 0.2 (from_interval(itvl, m, eps, gamma)) are tuned, not derived. eps trades initial-set inflation O(eps²) against conditioning 1/eps. gamma should track the expansion parameter; too small is not free — cond(α) degrades the refit and bounds faster than the drift gain (measured: gamma = 0.05 diverges earlier than 0.2); 0.2 is near the empirical crossing point on the VDP benchmarks.
Loose ends. (1) The seeded rows are not where nonconvexity comes from: rows evolve as row·(I+dtA)⁻¹ and keep their ε scale; the set curves because the weight-1 row’s polynomial picks up higher-degree components along the flow. (2) The whole choice is a gauge: a degree-scaled α in the unit-normalized basis equals an equal-weight α in an L-graded basis — the L-grading must live in the basis (monomial world, bad interval arithmetic), in the metric (this choice, mild cond cost), or nowhere (equal seeding, O(1)-priced truncation error).

5The symplectic step and the exact telescoping

Per step of size h, with A evaluated at the old center and frame:

α₊(I + hA) = α + hU   (implicit solve)
ox₊ = ox + h f(ox)

Along the lifted Euler step b₊ = (I + hA)b + hC_H b_hi the implicit choice telescopes exactly:

α₊ b₊ = α b + h( U b + α₊ C_H b_hi )

— the entire modeled transport cancels; only the hypercontrol U and the genuine residual drive the level. The y-update just bounds ‖αb + hG‖² over the set (§7). Explicit Euler on the adjoint (α₊ = α(I − hA)) instead leaves W = −hαA² in the bound: with ‖A‖ ≈ 50 on the lift this is an O(h‖A‖²) tax per unit time — measured ~4–5% looser tubes at the same h, ~5× smaller h to match, and a compounding pairing leak on linear systems (report, Figure 5). Pre-grading, this same term caused immediate divergence.

6The refit: recenter, reorient, renormalize (all exact)

At every step start, before anything else:

  • Slice frame. On the variety, b₀ ≡ 1, so the set’s linear marginal is the lifted ellipsoid {‖αv‖² ≤ y} intersected with the slice v₀ = 1 — an exact ellipsoid {μ + C⊥ξ : ‖ξ‖ ≤ s} with μ = Ca/‖a‖², s² = y − 1/‖a‖² (a, C = rows of α⁻¹). Using the slice matters: the plain ellipsoid projection is wrecked by the spectator rows’ 1/ε entries in α⁻¹.
  • Frame change. Map that marginal to the unit ball: M = sqrtm(s²C⊥C⊥ᶱ), then T ← M⁻¹T, ox ← ox + T⁻¹μ, and α ← αS where S is the exact change-of-basis matrix for b(μ + Mw) (an affine map preserves total degree, so S is computed exactly on the (m+1)-point grid). The set is unchanged; the basis now sees it centered, unit-scaled, and axis-aligned to its own geometry.
  • Renormalization. α ← α/c, y ← y/c² with c = ‖α‖_F/√|b| — same set, prevents scale drift over long horizons.

All three are set identities — they change the description, never the certified set, so they can run every step. This is also why the embedding is symplectic-only: the refit is inherently a discrete jump; a continuous ODE solver cannot represent it.

7The four offset bounds

All bound V₊ = ‖αb + hG‖², G = Wb + α₊C_H b_hi, over the set; y₊ is their minimum (each is individually sound). They differ in where exact algebra hands over to the interval step:

boundideauses the set?cost/step
Cauchy–Schwarz(√y + h‖G‖)², rowabs per component via ‖αb‖≤√y~0.01 ms
bilinearabs-sum of the quadratic-form matrices via V ≤ y (partly)~0.01 ms
scalar interpolationexpand 2(αb)ᶱG itself in Chebyshev, abs-sum its coefficients — keeps all product cancellations, near-tight box bound no~0.05 ms
partition (drift="partition", G cells/axis)Gram-style boundary cells on the static [-1,1]ⁿ box: prune cells whose natif(V) misses the level y, Lagrangian-projected mean-value bound on the rest yes — level set~3–9 ms

The measured hierarchy: the first three are near-optimal box bounds, but the drift sup lives on the set, and box/set = 3×…40× growing with degree. With graded weights that gap multiplies a geometrically small number, so interval-only suffices deep in the basin (and runs at L2-log-norm speed, 0.05–0.2 ms/step). Near the basin frontier the budget tightens and the partition is what completes the horizon (report, Figure 3: interval m=3 dies at t≈1.1; partition survives t=14 while the L2 log-norm dies at t≈5.4). The partition needs the gather-free basis evaluator (_cheb_eval_safe) because natif cannot interpret fancy indexing.

8Where the current limits are

  • Forward VDP / relaxation jump: near the jump |f″| is several times larger; the interval bounds start 50–100× over truth, the certificate inflates while the truth contracts, and past a point even the exact drift of the inflated set explodes. Partition is converged and still dies — not a bounding problem.
  • Ultra-thin sets (100:1 pancake): the bounds are provably exact there (partition = sampled truth), and the true drift itself blows up: y absorbs shape misfit measured in units of the thin direction, so aspect ratio multiplies the rate. Pure transport-misfit.
  • Both point at the same missing piece: the hypercontrol U (already plumbed through every bound, including the partition’s W term) or a transport law beyond the frozen Carleman adjoint — not more bounding.
